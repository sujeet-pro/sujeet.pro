Designing a URL Redirection & Vanity URL Service

Start with a config file. Earn every component after that.

Quick Read · 2 Min Principal Engineer Summary
  • Most teams should stop at L1–L2. A DB of record plus a cached map, pushed to edge KV when regions multiply, covers almost every marketing redirect estate.
  • Bloom filters only pay off at L3, when bot path-scans and 10M+ user-generated rules make origin misses the cost and availability problem.
  • L5 is a replacement, not an add-on. Multi-tenant pattern rules mean a new matcher with precedence and conflict detection, not another cache layer.
  • AI-era lens: throwaway rule migrators and shadow-replay verifiers are cheap now. The bottleneck is DNS cutover and slug ownership.

Assumptions Every number on this page (rules, rps, regions, miss %, cost bands) is an illustrative assumption to verify against current AWS pricing and quotas.

    AI-Era Build vs. Buy Lens

    Build vs. buy: owning L3 costs list infra plus ~0.25 FTE of on-call and slug governance; buying is cheaper below ~20M redirects/mo. Coding agents now draft the rule compiler, Terraform modules, and synthetic load tests in days rather than weeks (assumption: ~2–3× faster first drafts), which lowers the build cost but not the run cost. Unless custom auth, zero-hop edge latency, or data residency forces ownership, the deciding line item is on-call and DNS cutover governance — not infrastructure.
